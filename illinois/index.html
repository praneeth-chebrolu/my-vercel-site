How the index is built

1. Cancer burden

Real per-county annual case counts and age-adjusted incidence rates (state ~464/100k) from the Illinois State Cancer Registry / State Cancer Profiles (All Cancer Sites, 2018–2022, age-adjusted to the 2000 U.S. standard population). Load a different cancer type or year by re-running build_data.py.

2. Population & density

Real county population, land area, and density (U.S. Census). Geometry is the Census county boundary set, filtered to Illinois (FIPS 17).

3. Trial centers

Real Illinois oncology centers geocoded to their facilities. Active-trial and Phase I counts are illustrative placeholders — wire them to ClinicalTrials.gov for live volumes.

4. Geographic accessibility

For each county centroid we compute straight-line distance to every center, convert to a road estimate (×1.27) and drive time (~52 mph), then count centers and trials reachable within 30 / 60 / 90 minutes.

5. Access-to-Innovation Index

Index = 100 × √(burden × access-gap), where burden is normalized estimated cases and access-gap blends drive time (55%) and scarcity of nearby trials (45%). Higher = greater unmet need. It highlights counties where many people face cancer but the nearest research site is far.

Reproduce the data

Run python3 illinois/build_data.py after dropping real ISCR and ClinicalTrials.gov inputs in place. It regenerates data/counties.json.

Data provenance. Population, land area, density, and county geometry are real (U.S. Census). Cancer incidence — per-county annual case counts and age-adjusted rates — is real, from the Illinois State Cancer Registry / State Cancer Profiles (All Cancer Sites, 2018–2022). Cancer-center locations are real; per-center active-trial counts and drive times are modeled estimates for planning, not clinical or navigational advice. Built as a prototype of a Clinical Intelligence Engine / Access-to-Innovation Index.
